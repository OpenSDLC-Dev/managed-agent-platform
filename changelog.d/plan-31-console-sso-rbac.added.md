- **Plan 31 drafted — console SSO and RBAC** ([docs/plan/31_console-sso-rbac.md](docs/plan/31_console-sso-rbac.md), [#56](https://github.com/OpenSDLC-Dev/managed-agent-platform/issues/56)) — the design for the SSO + RBAC half of #56: today one `x-api-key` is the whole management authority, and this plan replaces that flat trust with humans authenticating through **any standards-compliant OIDC provider** while the control plane resolves each request to a principal holding one of three claim-mapped roles (`admin`/`developer`/`viewer`) enforced per route — the reference's own shape, where roles gate what humans may mint and the API server enforces per-credential authority. The core is a strict, vendor-SDK-free relying party with two modes sharing one verifier: generic OIDC RP, and trusted-proxy signed-header verification with a `gcp-iap` preset. **Casdoor ships as the hardened self-host default** (pinned ≥ v2.387.0, zero upstream providers, single org, token-exchange grant off, SAML routes blocked — the posture CERT/CC VU#780781 demands, priced in openly rather than hidden), replaceable by config with Keycloak or any cloud IdP. Every documented CLI/SDK flow and machine credential behaves identically throughout, and the browser flow never touches the platform; the identity lane's one observable `/v1` addition lands as a declared divergence. Settled with the user 2026-08-12 after research spanning the reference's console/CLI/SDK auth surfaces, the Casdoor CVE record verified to source, and the GCP/AWS adapter landscape. Six slices follow: verifier, principals + identity lane, the role matrix, deployment wiring, an observation-gated api-key surface, acceptance. Multi-tenant activation stays in #56, sized but not built.
