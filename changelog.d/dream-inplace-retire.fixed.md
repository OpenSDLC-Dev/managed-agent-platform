- **A dream's merge rules now say when a contradicted memory is retired rather than changed** — the rules let a dream change a memory that a transcript contradicts, or remove it, but did not say which. In an in-place dream, removing means retiring the memory as a tombstone. So a memory whose subject had ended could be rewritten as news of the end, and an in-place dream then listed nothing for the caller to remove. The rule now decides by the thing the memory's title or first line names. While that thing stands, the dream changes the memory. Once a transcript ends it, the dream first moves what still holds out of the memory, into the memory it belongs to, then removes or retires it. Stage 1 now asks its plan to list such a memory as a suspect, stage 3 asks to remove or retire it, and an in-place dream's stage 4 audit asks to retire one the merge left about an ended thing. The prompt also asks an in-place dream to list its tombstones in its index in one form: a `## To remove` heading, then one `- /<path>` line per tombstone, and no such heading when the store holds no tombstone; the model writes that listing, and nothing validates it. The in-place eval checks that listing, and that the successor fact survives outside the tombstone. (#1002)
